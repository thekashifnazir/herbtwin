# herbtwin: notes for judges

herbtwin looks at one photo of a herb or garden plant and says what it needs now: water
it, hold off, shelter it, treat it, or leave it because what you see is normal for the
season. It is built for UK gardeners with herbs outdoors.

**Address:** https://herbtwin.kashifnazir.com

## 1. Enter the judges' code (30 seconds)

The site caps photo reads per visitor per day. The judges' code skips the caps. The code
is in the submission form; it is not in this repository.

1. Open https://herbtwin.kashifnazir.com/judge
2. Type the code and press **Use code**.
3. The page says "You're in". The code is remembered on that browser for 60 days.

## 2. What to try in five minutes

1. **Read a photo.** On the home page choose a photo (yours, or a sample from section 3).
   A sample is set in London ("Sample photo: set in London"); to try another place, press
   **Use somewhere else** and type a UK town or postcode. A photo of your own that has its
   location says "Using your photo's location"; without one, type a town, or there is no
   weather and no test on the plant's twin. Press **What does my plant need?**
   (the big green button; **Analyse** on a site without the spot answer).
2. **Watch it work.** The page names the step it is on at the top and lists each step as it happens ("Reading the photo",
   "Found …", "Looking up how … likes to grow", "Checking the weather where you are",
   "Thinking about what it needs"), then opens the answer. Section 4 says why.
3. **Read the answer.** It comes in three blocks (on a wide screen the first is on the
   left, the other two on the right):
   - **What to do.** The plant, then where it grows, the date and the place as a row of
     chips ("Outdoors", "In a container", "18 Oct", "London"); with no place outdoors, the
     band asks "We don't know where this is: add a town for weather-checked advice." with a
     town box and a lime **Check the advice** button; the photo you sent, big beside
     the main card (under it on a phone, so the advice is in the first screen); when the photo's exposure let us measure it, the
     light at that moment ("Light at 14:05: part shade, about 40% of open-sky light"); then the main action in one
     big card with a green head: its tag (TODAY; no tag when the advice is to leave it),
     the headline, why, a "→" line saying what happens if you do it (when there is data
     on that), and "How sure we are" with a word badge, a green "Our best read" or an amber
     "Not sure, check first", and a plain line on what that meant in our tests: "Right
     about half the time in our tests" or "Right about 1 in 3 times in our tests". When it
     is not sure, the line under it says what lowered it most ("Mostly: only 1 of 3 advisors agreed."), and a green **Make
     this surer** button jumps to the questions that would help, or to the box to tell
     us more when no one fact would (a missing place is asked once, on the band, not
     again here; on a phone, the least sure answers put them under the card). A
     blurry, dark or distant photo the plant can't be named from says so above the
     headline, and **Send a sharper photo** (or brighter, or closer) is the green button
     then, with Make this surer beside it. Under
     the chips, "**Indoors? Switch and we'll redo the advice for an indoor plant**" (or
     the outdoor mirror) re-runs the advice indoors (no forecast) or outdoors. Under the main card, compact and
     coloured, with their names in words: **Also do** (green, tagged THIS WEEK),
     **Don't** (clay; headed **Check first**, "Water only if the top of the compost is
     dry", when we can't tell how wet the soil is) and **Keep an eye on** (amber, when there
     are signs to watch for); then, open, **See it by part**: the same lines sorted
     into pot, soil, leaves and stems, and where it sits, nothing new; then a
     **What next** card: send a new photo in a few days, **Add 1 or 2 more photos** of the same
     plant for a surer answer (when that is on), **I did it** for an action you can
     carry out, and **Send another photo**; under **Remove it. There is no cure for this.**
     it says to bag it and bin it, not the compost, and to check the plants beside it, with
     no more photos of a plant that is going. A pest the sources say to pick off by hand
     gets that as its headline ("Pick the beetles off by hand.") in place of "Treat it.";
   - **Tell us more and we'll check the advice.** Folded to one line, **Want a more exact
     answer? Tell us more**, until you tap it or a **Make this surer** button on the main
     card; it starts open once you have asked or told us something, or when the main card
     says to answer the question below. When a fact from you
     would make the advice surer, a yellow box says what would help. All in one panel,
     in small headed sections, each shown only when it has something in it: **Where it
     is** (the place box), **What's changed** (how the soil feels, when you last
     watered), then **Check the advice again**; **Ask a question** (one text box, **Ask
     about this plant**, three questions to tap for this plant, such as "Is it safe to
     eat?", and the replies); **Reminders**. With no questions to answer, both buttons
     sit under the text box;
   - **How we worked it out**: first, who advised, in plain words (today one model asked the
     same question three times; each answer is an "advisor"); then **What each advisor said**: each
     one's call in bold, in the page's words and never the advisor's own (so nothing the plan
     leaves out comes back this way), as one line when all three agree, with a note saying
     whose plan the top is ("Our rules then chose the plan at the top, so follow that." when
     a rule of ours led); then,
     when a rule of ours chose the lead, a note saying how
     ("The advisors led with …"); then, folded, **Why it's our best read** (or **Why
     we're not sure**: what helped (▲), what lowered it (▼), what was missing (?) and what made no
     difference (·), in words), **Compare all choices (next 2 weeks)** (outdoors, once we
     know where it is, or a line saying why the choices don't apply when there is no cure:
     each action
     against leaving it, tested on the plant's twin: a small water-and-frost model of the
     plant run over the next 2 weeks of weather) and **Weather, forecast and sources**
     (the evidence behind the answer).
4. **Refine.** Tap **Want a more exact answer? Tell us more**. Under "Tell us more and
   we'll check the advice", in **What's changed**, say how the soil feels
   2 cm down, when you last watered, or what changed, then press **Check the advice
   again** under it (anything typed in the box under **Ask a question** goes too). A changed headline shows the old one struck
   through. Five refines per answer.
5. **Ask about this plant.** Under **Ask a question**, type in the same box, for example "What if I
   move it inside?" or "What if I don't water it this week?", and press **Ask about this
   plant** (or Enter; Shift+Enter is a new line), or tap one of the three questions. The reply appears under the box and the advice stays as it is. What-ifs are
   answered by running the choice on the plant's twin; some answers cite gardening sites. Ten
   questions per answer. (The Ask button needs JavaScript; without it the box goes with
   **Check the advice again**.)
6. **Sign in** (top right of the home page). Type an email address and open the link
   sent to it; there is no password. Signed in, each answer is kept against a plant;
   open **My plants** to see them. When a new photo could be a plant you already have,
   the site asks "Which plant is this?" (or "A new plant").
7. **"Did you do it?"** Send a second photo of the same plant and pick it. When the
   second photo falls inside the main action's check window (1 to 7 days for watering, 3
   to 21 days for most other actions), the answer asks "On … we suggested …. Did you do
   it?" (under "Tell us more and we'll check the advice", headed **Your last advice**) and compares the two photos. The
   window starts a day after the first photo for watering advice, three days after for most
   other actions and a week after for feeding or more light, so this needs a photo on another day (or two photos whose camera dates
   are days apart).
   It will not appear in a five-minute session.
8. **Reminder email.** Signed in, an answer whose main action has a check window shows
   "Email me when it's time to check again". Ticked, one email is sent halfway through
   that window. The email has a link to stop it.
9. **What can I grow here?** Press the button of that name on the home page, beside
   **What does my plant need?** (it is not in the menu, and does not show on a site without the spot
   answer). Choose a photo of a bed, pot or border with the soil in view. Sample 7 in section 3 (`7-pea-bed.jpg`) is a bed; the others are close-ups of one plant, not spots. Type a UK
   town or postcode and send it. The page first says what it read
   (setting, month, place, sun). It then lists crops under **Good now** and **Worth a
   try**, with **Coming up** and **Not here** folded under **The details**. It may ask up to two quick questions, which way the
   spot faces and a soil squeeze test, to make the list surer. If it asks "Is it in a pot
   or in the ground?", tap one; otherwise, if the setting is wrong, change it under "Not
   right? Change the setting".

The [privacy page](https://herbtwin.kashifnazir.com/privacy) says what is kept and who
processes what. You can delete your account from My plants.

**Answers change with the date and the weather.** The advice is for this plant, in this
place, over the next 2 weeks, so the same photo can get a different answer on another day
or in another town (a sheltering answer in a cold week, say). The same photo, place and
question on the same day, signed out, usually gets the same answer again (a read that
hit a problem, such as no weather, is made afresh).

## 4. How long a read takes, and why it streams

A photo read makes four or five model calls: one to read the photo, a second look when the
read suggests a pest or disease, and three to the advisor (the same question asked three
times, to see whether the answers agree). The trusted-page check reads up to six sentences
a page from the first three trusted pages with Nemotron 3 Ultra, each with a second short
pass when it has conditions, and one last Ultra check on any sentence the code agrees with,
run while the advisors think. If GLM fails, the backup reads the photo too. Around them it fetches the
weather and the plant's care facts; a plant outside the built-in list has its RHS page
looked up and read. A read takes about 15 to 35 seconds: on the live site, 25 and 36
seconds on 14 Oct, 28 on 16 Oct and 15.5 on 19 Oct. (On 12 Oct, before the second look was
added, reads took 12 to 27 seconds, and one phone read took 52.) Checking the advice again
after a refine took about 5 seconds on 12 Oct. The site keeps one copy running all the
time, so there is no cold start.

The site sits behind a Cloudflare Worker, which drops a connection after about 125
seconds with no bytes sent. So the page sends a line per step, and a hidden keep-alive
every 10 seconds while a step is quiet, then jumps to the answer. Keep the page open. On a
weak mobile signal a full-size photo can take a while to upload, and a phone that switches
between Wi-Fi and mobile data can drop the page; the read still finishes, and a signed-in
answer appears in **My plants**.

## 5. Known limits

- **One app instance** serves everyone, so reads at a busy moment can be slower.
- **Daily caps:** 200 photo reads a day for the whole site and 10 per visitor (UK days).
  Over a cap you get "The garden's had a busy day. Try again tomorrow." The judges' code
  skips both, and also skips the stop when the site's model budget runs out.
- **UK, outdoors, herbs.** Season norms are UK-only; a place outside the UK gets a note
  and no season norm. The weather and the plant's twin are for plants outside.
- **Indoor light is not measured.** A plant indoors or on a windowsill gets no plant
  model, and the advice leans on the photo and the care facts.
- **A photo is not a soil test.** Soil moisture is guessed from the surface; the refine
  questions (how the soil feels 2 cm down) are there to correct it.
- **Models:** the photo is read by GLM-5.3-Flash (`zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash`) and the advice
  is written by NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super (`nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b`), both on
  Nebius Token Factory. If GLM fails or gives no reply within 90 s, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash
  (`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, also on Token Factory) reads the photo instead; it
  names the species a little less often (see `docs/reference/photo-model-fallback.md`).
  The models can be wrong about the species; the answer says how
  sure it is.

## 6. Where Nebius is used

- **Token Factory** runs every language and vision model call: reading the photo, the
  advisor, refine, chat and the "Did you do it?" comparison.
- **Nebius AI Cloud** runs the daily reminder job as a Serverless Job, started each
  morning by a GitHub Actions workflow from an image in Nebius Container Registry. See
  [`docs/REMINDERS.md`](docs/REMINDERS.md).

The site itself runs on Google Cloud Run in London, behind Cloudflare. Accounts and
history are in Supabase; sign-in and reminder emails go through Resend.
