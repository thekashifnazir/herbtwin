herbtwin, and why I built it

This autumn my oregano died and my purple sage nearly did, and I could not tell what was normal for October. Most plant apps name the plant and list generic care; they rarely say "leave it", or how sure they are. herbtwin is for UK growers with herbs outdoors: the season norms, frost data and soil maps are all UK.

What it does

  1. You send a photo and a place (a town, a postcode or the photo's own location). Up to 3 photos of one plant count as one read.
  2. It tries each choice on the plant's twin: a small water-and-frost model, run over the next 2 weeks of your weather. Indoors there is no forecast test.
  3. Three advisors weigh it: NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super, asked three times on its own. How far they agree feeds how sure the answer is.
  4. You get one thing to do first, and how sure we are: "our best read, about half the time" or "not sure, check first, about 1 in 3". In about a fifth of expert answers the right advice is "leave it".

Under the answer you can ask about the plant or check the advice again; signed in, you get an email when it is time to check, and the next photo asks "Did you do it?".{% if spot_on %} What can I grow here? takes a photo of a bed and lists what to sow or plant this month.{% endif %} No account needed; a read takes 15 to 35 seconds, with daily caps (see Privacy).

  4. The twin: 6 choices × 14 days

    6. {% if check_on %}
  6. Trusted-page check
    7. {% endif %}
  7. The answer, streamed

Python 3.12 and FastAPI. One adapter over Nebius Token Factory carries every model call. "Is it normal now?" comes from 360 cited month-by-month rows for 30 plants. Reminders run as a Nebius AI Cloud Serverless Job; the public eval reports sit in Nebius Object Storage.

What an answer costs

Every answer lists which model did each step, its time and its cost. One example read (basil, 21 Oct): GLM-5.3-Flash read the photo (17.4 s, $0.0016), three Nemotron 3 Super advisors weighed it (6.0 s, $0.0024): $0.0040 of model time, against about $0.0177 had every step run on Nemotron 3 Ultra.

One example, not an average. The Ultra figure is an estimate: the same tokens at Ultra's listed price.

The expert questions are text only, so they score Nemotron without the twin. Several tests were pre-registered, and more than 3,700 automated tests run before every push (how we measured).

An expert's review. A paid professional gardener, with over 3 years' hands-on experience, graded the advice on 3 openly licensed photos: all three first actions Acceptable, 3 out of 5 overall. Four rules changed because of it, and "7 in 10" was retired once we measured it at 51% on 624 expert questions. Three photos, one expert, not graded again (the review).

A real user. A former tree surgeon asked where to get the things the answer tells you to use. That became the shops card under the answer.

What didn't work

  • Specialist advisors failed all three rules they were judged on (774 expert questions), so the three asks stay plain.
  • The trusted-page check failed four pre-registered rounds before the fifth passed, then backed nothing on real pages (round 6). Round 7 failed (4 false agrees in 205) and didn't ship. Round 8 shipped: 3 false agrees in 418, under our 3% bar, but it finds 10 of the 26 quotes that should show, short of its 50% target.
  • Ultra as the advisor over-treated; "7 in 10" overstated how sure we were.
  • Nano on our own GPU agreed with Token Factory on 81% of answers but matched the expert 4.8 points less often.
  • Cosmos video did not show the change asked for, where an image editor did (5 of 6).
  • Prices for the things to buy: a Nemotron 3 Nano reader got 11 of 21 right, 9 wrong, and 4 of 6 shops block a direct fetch, so we show the item and the shop, not a price.

What's next

  • A twin for indoor plants, and a Nemotron photo reader (Nemotron 3.5 Super VL first).
  • Naming more problems: the check still doesn't name slugs.
  • A real screen-reader test, a beginners' user test, and more than one server.

Accessibility

axe-core (WCAG 2.0 to 2.2, A and AA) on the main pages, in light and dark, found one miss: a dark-mode "(optional)" at 3.98:1, now 5.4:1, with a test. Every keyboard stop shows a focus ring, buttons are at least 44 px, nothing scrolls sideways at 320 px, and motion is off under Reduce Motion. Not yet done: a real screen reader (the checks).
