About

herbtwin, and why I built it

This autumn my oregano died and my purple sage nearly did, and I couldn't tell what was normal for October. Most plant apps name the plant and list generic care; they rarely say "leave it", or how sure they are. herbtwin does.

What an answer costs

Every answer lists which model did each step, its time and its cost. One example read (basil, 21 Oct): $0.0040 of model time, against about $0.0177 had every step run on Nemotron 3 Ultra.

A few decisions I'd call out

  • The mid-size model decides, not the biggest. Nemotron 3 Ultra, the biggest, said "treat it" when the expert did not in 12.7% of answers; Super, the middle size, in 2%.
  • The small model runs the chat. Nemotron 3 Nano: 10 of 10 on our what-if check, in about half Super's time.
  • A non-NVIDIA model reads the photo, because it named plants better. Four NVIDIA vision models named the plant 0.54 to 0.627 of the time, against GLM-5.3-Flash's 0.846 to 0.867 (the details).

What didn't work

  • More advisors didn't mean better advice. Specialist advisors (season, soil and water, pests and health) failed all three rules on 774 expert questions. Now: three plain advisors, asked the same question.
  • A quote is only worth showing if it's right. The trusted-page check failed rounds 1 to 4 and 7 (4 false agrees in 205). Round 8 shipped: 3 false agrees in 418, under our 3% bar. Now: it stays quiet unless sure, finding 10 of the 26 quotes that should show (target 50%).
  • Our own GPU was dearer and less right. Nano on a Nebius GPU agreed with Token Factory on 81% of answers but matched the expert 4.8 points less often, at about $1.63 a run. Now: every model runs on Token Factory.
  • Video models can't show "before and after" yet. Cosmos didn't show the change asked for; an image editor did (5 of 6). Now: still pictures, drawn ahead of time.
  • Shops don't give prices to robots. A Nemotron 3 Nano price reader got 11 of 21 right, 9 wrong and 1 left blank; 4 of 6 shops block a direct fetch. Now: we show the item and the shop, not a price.

What's next

  • A twin for indoor plants. Why: indoors there's no forecast test yet. We now ask which window a plant is by; next, the twin models it.
  • A Nemotron photo reader, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL first. Why: 5 times faster than GLM, so every step runs on NVIDIA. It's behind on naming plants today.
  • Naming more problems, starting with slugs. Why: slugs are a common herb pest, and the photo check can't name them yet.
  • Tests with real people. Why: so far it's been checked by tools, one paid expert and two gardeners. Next: a screen-reader test and a beginners' test.
  • More than one server. Why: one server and a daily cap today.

Who made it

I'm Kash, a Senior Technical Architect based in London. My day job is cloud, AI strategy and enterprise modernisation, where I set direction and prove out ideas far more often than I write the code myself.

herbtwin inverts that: one idea, one person, built end to end for the NVIDIA × Nebius hackathon (I was at the London event). I lean on AI heavily to do it and say so plainly; the architecture and the judgement calls are mine. The first commit is 2 Oct 2026.
