About

herbtwin, and why I built it

This autumn my oregano died and my purple sage nearly did, and I couldn't tell what was normal for October. Most plant apps name the plant and list generic care; they rarely say "leave it", or how sure they are. herbtwin does.

Check a plant

What it does

Photo a herb and say where it grows. In 15 to 35 seconds, with no account, you get one thing to do and how sure we are. It is for UK growers with herbs outdoors: the season norms, frost data and soil maps are all UK. Signed in, you get an email when it's time to look again, and the next photo asks "Did you do it?".{% if spot_on %} What can I grow here? takes a photo of a bed and lists what to sow or plant this month.{% endif %}

What an answer costs

Every answer lists which model did each step, its time and its cost. One example read (basil, 21 Oct): $0.0040 of model time, against about $0.0177 had every step run on Nemotron 3 Ultra.

One example, not an average. The Ultra figure is an estimate: the same tokens at Ultra's listed price.

How it's built

  • Python 3.12
  • FastAPI
  • Cloud Run
  • Cloudflare Worker
  • Nebius Token Factory
  • NVIDIA Nemotron 3
  • Tavily
  • Open-Meteo
  • GBIF
  • Supabase

One adapter carries every model call to Nebius Token Factory; reminders run as a Nebius AI Cloud Serverless Job.

A few decisions I'd call out

  • Super decides, not Ultra. Ultra, the largest, over-treated: 12.7% against Super's 2%.
  • Nano talks. The chat, 10 of 10 on our what-if check, in about half Super's time.
  • GLM-5.3-Flash reads the photo. Four NVIDIA vision models named the plant less often: 0.54 to 0.627, against 0.846 to 0.867 (the details).

The expert questions are text only, so they score Nemotron without the twin. More than 4,000 automated tests run before every push (how we measured).

An expert's review. A paid professional gardener graded 3 openly licensed photos: every first action Acceptable, 3 of 5 overall. Four rules changed, and "7 in 10" was retired after we measured 51% on 624 expert questions. One expert, three photos (the review).

A real user. A former tree surgeon asked where to get the things the answer tells you to use. That became the shops card under the answer.

What didn't work

  • Specialist advisors failed all three rules they were judged on (774 expert questions), so the three asks stay plain.
  • The trusted-page check failed rounds 1 to 4; round 5's pass backed nothing on real pages; round 7 failed (4 false agrees in 205). Round 8 shipped: 3 false agrees in 418, under our 3% bar, but it finds 10 of the 26 quotes that should show (target 50%).
  • Nano on our own GPU agreed with Token Factory on 81% of answers but matched the expert 4.8 points less often.
  • Cosmos video didn't show the change asked for; an image editor did (5 of 6).
  • Prices for the things to buy failed a zero-wrong bar: a Nemotron 3 Nano reader got 11 of 21 right, 9 wrong and 1 left blank, and 4 of 6 shops block a direct fetch, so we show the item and the shop, not a price.

What's next

  • A twin for indoor plants, and a Nemotron photo reader (Nemotron 3.5 Super VL first).
  • Naming more problems: the check still doesn't name slugs.
  • A real screen-reader test, a beginners' user test, and more than one server.

Accessibility

axe-core (WCAG 2.2 AA) on the main pages, light and dark: one miss, a dark-mode "(optional)" at 3.98:1, now 5.4:1. Focus rings on every stop, 44 px buttons, no sideways scroll at 320 px, motion off under Reduce Motion (the checks).

Who made it

I'm Kash, a Senior Technical Architect based in London. My day job is cloud, AI strategy and enterprise modernisation, where I set direction and prove out ideas far more often than I write the code myself.

herbtwin inverts that: one idea, one person, built end to end for the NVIDIA × Nebius hackathon (I was at the London event). I lean on AI heavily to do it and say so plainly; the architecture and the judgement calls are mine. The first commit is 2 Oct 2026.

All of it is public. The code, the eval reports and the Devpost story.
