About

herbtwin, and why I built it

This autumn my oregano died and my purple sage nearly did, and I couldn't tell what was normal for October. Most plant apps name the plant and list generic care; they rarely say "leave it", or how sure they are. herbtwin does. It works best for herbs grown outside.

Checked by people

An expert's review. A professional gardener hired through Tendem graded 3 photos: every first action Acceptable, 3 of 5 overall.
Four rules changed, and "7 in 10" went once we measured 51% (the review, how we measured).

A real user. A former tree surgeon asked where to get what the answer tells you to use.
That became the shops card under the answer.

A small Toloka pilot to check our photo reading against people: beginners caught the named problem in 4 of 5 photos, but seemed to over-call with a healthy photo beside it (3 of 5). A tiny sample: 5 photos, 15 raters.

A first-time-user study on Toloka (23 Oct, 11 people): all got an answer, but only 6 could say what to do, so the answer page now leads with the action (the plan, the results). Tendem and Toloka are paid from Nebius AI Builder programme credits.

What didn't work

  • More advisors didn't mean better advice. Specialist advisors (season, soil and water, pests and health) failed all three rules on 774 expert questions. Now: one model, Nemotron 3 Super, asked the same question three times.
  • A quote is only worth showing if it's right. The trusted-page check failed rounds 1 to 4 and 7 (4 false agrees in 205). Round 8 shipped: 3 false agrees in 418, under our 3% bar. Now: it stays quiet unless sure, finding 10 of the 26 quotes that should show (target 50%).
  • Our own GPU was dearer and less right. Nano on a Nebius GPU agreed with Token Factory on 81% of answers but matched the expert 4.8 points less often, at about $1.63 a run. Now: every model runs on Token Factory.
  • Video models can't show "before and after" yet. Cosmos didn't show the change asked for; an image editor did (5 of 6). Now: still pictures, drawn ahead of time.
  • Shops don't give prices to robots. A Nemotron 3 Nano price reader got 11 of 21 right, 9 wrong and 1 left blank; 4 of 6 shops block a direct fetch. Now: we show the item and the shop, not a price.

What's next

  1. A twin for indoor plants. Why: indoors there's no forecast test yet. We now ask which window a plant is by; next, the twin models it.
  2. A Nemotron photo reader, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL first. Why: then reading the photo runs on NVIDIA too, and it's 5 times faster than GLM. It's behind on naming plants today.
  3. Naming more problems, starting with slugs. Why: slugs are a common herb pest, and the photo check can't name them yet.
  4. Tests with real people. Why: no screen-reader test yet, and the beginners' study ran before the answer page changed (see Checked by people).
  5. More than one server. Why: one server and a daily cap today.
