Privacy

herbtwin looks at a photo of a plant and says what it needs. This page says what that involves in plain words.

Check a plant

What is kept

  • Signed in: your photos, your plants, the answers and the chat under them are kept in Supabase (hosted in the EU) so the app can remember your plants.
  • Signed out: nothing is kept on disk. The answer, and the photo as the AI saw it (no location data), stay in the server's memory for an hour, so you can ask follow-up questions or add more photos for a surer answer, then they are gone.
  • The answer page shows your photo back from a small copy kept in your own browser tab. It is not sent again and goes when you close the tab.
  • Locations are rounded to about 1 km before anything uses or keeps them.
  • What can I grow here? reads your photo once for the setting and the soil and keeps no photo. The way the spot faces is the direction you pick; nothing reads your phone's compass. Signed in, the spot's answer and the direction you picked are kept with the rounded place, so a later photo there can reuse it.
  • To keep the site within its limits we count photo reads per visitor per day. The visitor is stored as a scrambled code made from your internet address, never the address itself.

Cookies

Only the ones the site needs to work, none for tracking or adverts:

  • Signed in: two sign-in cookies keep you signed in, one for an hour and one for 30 days. Signing out removes them.
  • Judges: entering the judges' code sets one cookie for 60 days, so the daily photo limits don't apply in that browser.
  • Signed out and without the code, the site sets no cookies.

Who processes what

  • Nebius Token Factory runs the AI models that read your photo and write the advice. The photo is sent with its location data stripped.
  • LangSmith keeps a record of the AI model calls (the text sent and returned, timings and cost) so we can see how the site is doing and which advice worked. It gets no photos, email addresses or locations, only place names.
  • Supabase stores signed-in accounts and their plants.
  • Resend sends the sign-in emails, and a reminder email if you tick "Email me when it's time to check again" under an answer: one email to your sign-in address for that answer. The link in it, or unticking the box, stops it.
  • Open-Meteo gets a rounded location to look up the weather and a place's coldest winter nights. It also gets a town name you type, to find where it is.
  • postcodes.io gets a UK postcode you type, to find roughly where it is. It is told nothing else.
  • Open Plantbook gets the plant's name, to look up the light and soil moisture it likes. It is told nothing else.
  • Web search services (Tavily, Exa, TinyFish) get a search built from the plant's name or your chat question when the app looks something up on gardening sites. Tavily also gets the kind of thing that helps with the advice (for example, horticultural fleece), to find it at UK garden shops; nothing about you goes with it. If you typed a UK town or postcode, Tavily gets that town's name, to find pages that list gardeners there. To show news under "Seen recently in the UK", Tavily gets the name of a pest or disease the plant can get (for example, rosemary beetle), to find this month's UK reports of it; nothing about you or where you are goes with it.

  • Cloudflare and Google Cloud carry and run the site.
  • Nebius AI Cloud runs the daily reminder job: for each reminder due, it reads your sign-in email address, the plant's name and the action from Supabase and hands the email to Resend.

Deleting your account

Signed in, go to My plants and choose Delete account. Your photos, plants, answers and sign-in are deleted.
