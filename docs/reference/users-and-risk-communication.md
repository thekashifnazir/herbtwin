# Users, risk communication and trust

Evidence on who uses a plant-care assistant and how to present its advice, to support the
"potential impact" and "design" criteria and the 14-day risk strip. Swept 3 Oct 2026.
Every DOI was resolved through CrossRef or OpenAlex. **[A]** means the finding was read
from the abstract or page that day; **[M]** means it comes from memory of the paper and
was not re-read, so treat it as unverified. No full texts were read.

## 1. Why plants are lost, and market size

- **Clemson Cooperative Extension, Indoor Plants: Watering**, hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/. Names over-watering as the leading killer of potted plants, and says that over- and under-watering symptoms look alike [A]. This is expert assertion, not a survey. **No empirical study quantifying causes of houseplant death was found. Say "extension services state", never "studies show".**
- **HTA Garden Industry Statistics**, hta.org.uk/news-events-current-issues/industry-data/garden-industry-statistics. 77% of British adults have a private garden (about 44 million); 58% of those grow plants (about 26 million); 31% grow their own herbs, fruit or vegetables; about £9bn retail garden spend in 2025 [A].
- **National Gardening Survey 2023**, gardenresearch.com. 80% of US households took part in lawn and garden activity in 2022, a 5-year high; average spend $616; houseplants were the fastest-growing activity; younger households were more often first-timers [A]. Novices are the growth segment.
- Knuth, Khachatryan, Hall 2021, Behavioral Sciences, doi:10.3390/bs11050073. Houseplant purchase research exists [A, weak].
- Not found: replacement-plant spending, time spent gardening, UK houseplant ownership rate, any RHS survey figure, a sourced age breakdown of gardeners. Do not quote one.

## 2. Use of plant apps

- **Hart et al. 2023, Assessing the accuracy of free automated plant identification applications, People and Nature, doi:10.1002/pan3.10460.** Five free apps, 857 images, 277 species: 69% correct at first suggestion, 85% within the top five [A]. Show top candidates with a confirm step; never condition care advice on one unconfirmed identification.
- Truong and van der Wal 2024, BioScience, doi:10.1093/biosci/biae077. Free and paid identification apps differ in presentation and data use [A]. Be transparent about data use.
- **Duda 2024, Urban gardening education: user reflections on mobile application designs, PLOS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0310357.** Analysis of 7,980 Play Store reviews of 14 gardening apps [A]. The best single source for what users want; worth one full read.
- Chang et al. 2022, Patterns and opportunities for the design of human-plant interaction, DIS, doi:10.1145/3532106.3533555. Survey of the field [A].
- Not found: any peer-reviewed study of care-app abandonment, or of fixed-schedule watering reminders versus condition-based advice. The criticism of fixed schedules is horticulturally sound but uncited; present it as our argument, supported by the adaptive-intervention literature in section 5.

## 3. Risk and forecast communication

- **Gigerenzer et al. 2005, "A 30% chance of rain tomorrow", Risk Analysis, doi:10.1111/j.1539-6924.2005.00608.x.** Single-event probabilities are read in contradictory ways because the reference class is unstated [A].
- **Joslyn and LeClerc 2012, Uncertainty forecasts improve weather-related decisions, J Exp Psych Applied, doi:10.1037/a0025185.** In a freezing-temperature road-salting task, numeric uncertainty improved decisions and raised trust compared with deterministic forecasts or advice alone [A]. The closest analogue to our frost decision.
- **LeClerc and Joslyn 2015, The cry wolf effect, Risk Analysis, doi:10.1111/risa.12336.** Decisions got worse when false alarms were either very frequent or very rare [A]. Tune thresholds; do not alert on every marginal day.
- Budescu, Broomell, Por 2009, Psychological Science, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02284.x; Wintle et al. 2019, PLOS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0213522. Verbal probability terms are read very variably [A]; pairing words with numbers narrows this [M].
- Padilla et al. 2021, Frontiers in Psychology, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.579267. Separates quantitative uncertainty from qualitative forecaster confidence [A]; both affect decisions [M].
- Broad et al. 2007, Misinterpretations of the cone of uncertainty, BAMS, doi:10.1175/bams-88-5-651. The cone is misread [A], typically as a hard boundary [M].
- Fernandes et al. 2018, CHI, doi:10.1145/3173574.3173718; Kay et al. 2016, CHI, doi:10.1145/2858036.2858558; Galesic et al. 2009, Health Psychology, doi:10.1037/a0014474. Frequency-framed displays (quantile dot plots, icon arrays) improve decisions, including for low-numeracy users [M].
- Not found: direct guidance on multi-day risk strips. The rules in section 7 are inferred.

## 4. Advice taking and trust in AI

- **Dietvorst, Simmons, Massey 2014, JEP General, doi:10.1037/xge0000033.** People abandon algorithms after seeing them err, even when they outperform humans [A]. One unwarned frost costs disproportionate trust.
- Dietvorst, Simmons, Massey 2018, Management Science, doi:10.1287/mnsc.2016.2643. People use imperfect algorithms if they can modify them slightly [M].
- Logg, Minson, Moore 2019, OBHDP, doi:10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.005. Lay people often weight algorithmic advice above human advice [M]. Novices may over-rely.
- Bansal et al. 2021, CHI, doi:10.1145/3411764.3445717; Buçinca, Malaya, Gajos 2021, CSCW, arXiv:2102.09692. Explanations did not reliably reduce overreliance [A] and can raise acceptance of wrong advice [M].
- Vasconcelos et al. 2023, CSCW, doi:10.1145/3579605. Explanations reduce overreliance when they make checking cheaper than doing the task [M].
- Zhang, Liao, Bellamy 2020, FAT*, doi:10.1145/3351095.3372852. Confidence scores helped trust calibration more than explanations [M].
- **Kim et al. 2024, FAccT, doi:10.1145/3630106.3658941.** N = 404: first-person uncertainty ("I'm not sure, but") lowered agreement and confidence in the system but raised accuracy by cutting overreliance [A].
- van der Bles et al. 2020, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1913678117. Five experiments, N = 5,633 [A]; numeric ranges cost little trust, verbal hedges more [M].
- Rose et al. 2016, Decision support tools for agriculture, Agricultural Systems, doi:10.1016/j.agsy.2016.09.009. Uptake depends on ease of use, relevance, trust and fit with workflow [M].

## 5. Actionability

- Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006, doi:10.1016/s0065-2601(06)38002-1. If-then plans have a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d about 0.65) [M].
- Stawarz, Cox, Blandford 2015, CHI, doi:10.1145/2702123.2702230. Reminders support repetition but hinder habit formation; cues tied to routines work better [M].
- **Nahum-Shani et al. 2017, Just-in-time adaptive interventions, Annals of Behavioral Medicine, doi:10.1007/s12160-016-9830-8.** Support should adapt to changing state and context [A]. The citable case for condition-based over fixed-schedule prompts.
- Fogg 2009, doi:10.1145/1541948.1541999; Mehrotra et al. 2016, CHI, doi:10.1145/2858036.2858566. A prompt works only when ability and motivation are present [M]. One small action per prompt; stay silent when nothing is needed.

## 6. Accessibility

- **Crameri, Shephard, Heron 2020, The misuse of colour in science communication, Nature Communications, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7.** Rainbow and red-green scales distort data and fail colour-vision deficiency [A]. No red and green traffic lights.
- Nurgalieva et al. 2019, IEEE Access, doi:10.1109/access.2019.2898467; Gomez-Hernandez et al. 2022, doi:10.2196/preprints.43186. Guidelines for older touchscreen users: larger targets and text, simple navigation [M]. Large day cells, no hover-only detail.

## 7. Design rules for the risk strip and the recommendation card

1. **Words plus numbers with a reference class.** Each day shows a label and a figure: "Frost likely, 7 in 10 nights like this" (Gigerenzer; Budescu; Joslyn 2012).
2. **Two separate channels.** Hazard probability and system confidence are encoded differently: fill for risk, a hatched or faded cell for low confidence, fading toward day 14, no hard-edged cone (Padilla; Broad).
3. **Colour-blind-safe scale.** One sequential, perceptually uniform ramp per hazard, with an icon and text duplicating the colour (Crameri).
4. **Alert thresholds tuned for false alarms.** Fire on meaningful risk only, and show past hits and misses openly (LeClerc and Joslyn; Dietvorst 2014).
5. **One if-then action per card.** Timed, tied to a cue, with a cheap physical check ("finger 2 cm into the soil: dry?") and an override such as "already watered" or "plant is under cover" (Gollwitzer; Vasconcelos; Dietvorst 2018).
6. **First-person hedge when confidence is low,** with the reason ("I'm not sure, the forecast beyond day 7 is weak"). Explanation text is never a substitute for a confidence figure (Kim; Zhang; Bansal).

These line up with the eval: rule 5's physical check is the differential-diagnosis question
in `differential-diagnosis.md`, and rule 6 is the "uncertainty stated" rubric line.

## 8. Impact figures we can quote

- **UK:** about 44 million adults (77%) have a private garden, about 26 million grow plants in it, 31% grow their own herbs, fruit or vegetables, and households spent about £9bn on retail garden products in 2025 (HTA Garden Industry Statistics).
- **US:** 80% of households took part in lawn and garden activities in 2022, spending $616 on average, with houseplants the fastest-growing activity (National Gardening Survey 2023).
- **Identification accuracy:** free plant-ID apps are right at first suggestion 69% of the time and 85% within the top five (Hart et al. 2023).
- Usable only as an expert statement, in our words: Clemson Extension states that over-watering is the leading killer of potted plants.
