## Per-plant detail

**Sage, Salvia officinalis (and 'Purpurascens', same rating).** Water low; drought-tolerant once established; a wet, cold winter in badly drained soil can kill it (W). Full sun (P). H5 (R: rhs.org.uk/plants/16356, /153600). October to November: evergreen subshrub, holds its grey leaves; plants go woody and straggly after 4 to 7 years (W, P, R). Problems: root or foot rot in wet compost (yellowing, collapse); powdery mildew (white dusty coating); rosemary beetle, a metallic striped 5 to 8 mm beetle that chews foliage (W, Chrysolina americana); capsid bug (tattered distorted leaves); sage leafhopper (pale mottling) (R). Pruning: trim mid to late spring after frosts, cut flower spikes after flowering, never into bare old wood. A flat-topped spring-cut plant is normal (P, R).

**Rosemary, Salvia rosmarinus.** Water low; withstands drought (W); dislikes heavy soil and winter wet; survives −10 to −15 °C (P); RHS says established plants survive −8 °C and pots freeze more readily. Full sun. H4 (R: /384211). October to November: evergreen, may flower in autumn (R). Problems: root rot from waterlogging is the most serious (R); frost damage browns new tips; rosemary beetle; leafhopper mottling; scale as waxy bumps on stems; cuckoo spit is harmless. Pruning: light trim after flowering, never into old wood; harvest at most 20% of growth (W). Kc: 0.16 in January to 1.13 in August, Iranian lysimeter study, Noorollahi et al. 2016 (idj.iaid.ir/article_55274_en.html; cite, licence unstated).

**Tarragon, Artemisia dracunculus (French).** Water low; drought-tolerant; killed in wet winters (P). Sun or light shade (P). H4 (R: /1632); French tarragon needs winter dryness or shelter. October to November: herbaceous; the first hard frost kills the top growth, and new shoots come in spring (R). Brown dead stems are normal. Problems: winter waterlogging, powdery mildew, rust (R). Cut dead stems to the base in spring.

**Oregano, Origanum vulgare.** Water low; dislikes wet soils (P). Full sun or part shade. H6 (R: /11923). October to November: woody-based perennial; flowered stems brown and leaves thin while a low basal rosette persists. Scruffy is normal; cut old stems in early spring (R). Problems: mint rust (orange pustules), mint moth caterpillars, leafhopper mottling (R), winter wet root rot.

**Garlic chives, Allium tuberosum.** Water medium; tolerates drought once established; USDA zones 4 to 10 (W). Sun, tolerates some shade (P). H5 (R: /877). October to November: leaves and stalks die back completely to the ground and resprout in spring (W). Problems: allium rust (bright orange pustules), allium leaf miner, onion white rot, downy mildew, slugs (R). Cut the whole clump to 5 cm after flowering (R).

**Parsley, Petroselinum crispum.** Water medium to high; moist well-drained soil (W, P); pots dry fast. Sun or part shade. H6 (R: /41248). Biennial: year-1 rosette, year-2 flower then death (W). October to November: growth slows, outer leaves yellow, survives a mild London winter; a spring-sown plant running up a flower stalk now is bolting and the end of the crop. Problems: bolting, celery leaf-mining fly (scorched brown blotches in the leaf), carrot fly, aphids, slugs, leaf spot, viruses (R). Harvest outer stems at the base (R).

**Mint, Mentha spicata.** Water high; prefers moist soil, tolerates heavy clay (P); shade-tolerant. H7 (R: /11055). October to November: herbaceous, stems brown and die back to the rhizome; a bare pot is normal; cut faded stems late autumn to spring (R). Problems: mint rust, Puccinia menthae, pale distorted spring shoots, then dusty orange pustules on stems and leaves followed by yellow or black pustules and leaf drop (W, R); blue mint beetle, mint moth, aphids, leafhoppers, powdery mildew. Cut to 5 cm after flowering (R). Kc: FAO-56 mint 0.60 / 1.15 / 1.10, height 0.6 to 0.8 m.

**Lemon balm, Melissa officinalis.** Water medium; drought-tolerant once established (P); sun or part shade. H7 (R: /11040). October to November: the stems die down as winter begins and regrow in spring (W). Problems: powdery mildew (white powder, common in late summer), root rot in cold soggy winter conditions, leafhoppers, heavy self-seeding (R). Cut hard after flowering; stubble is normal (R).

**Thyme, Thymus vulgaris.** Water low; tolerates about −15 °C; dislikes wet winter conditions, gravel mulch helps (P). Full sun only. H5 (R: /18213). October to November: evergreen, may look greyer and sparser; woody centre with age. Problems: root rot in damp winters, rosemary beetle, leafhoppers, slugs on young shoots (R). Trim after flowering or in spring, never into old wood (R).

**Basil, Ocimum basilicum.** Water medium; hates wet roots overnight (R); well drained. Full sun. H1c, frost-tender; outdoors only when nights stay above 10 °C (R). October to November in London: dying. Blackened limp leaves after cold nights, and flowering on woody stems, mean the plant is finishing, not a care fault; discarded in autumn (R). Problems: downy mildew, Peronospora belbahrii, angular yellow areas on the upper leaf with purple-brown mould beneath, worst near 20 °C with dewy nights, untreatable (W); fusarium wilt (wilting, vascular browning, W); grey mould, damping off, aphids, slugs (R). Pinch tips and flower spikes (W, R). Kc: 0.71 / 1.11 / 1.39 (initial, development, mid), Ghamarnia et al. 2014, Iran (cite only).

**Coriander, Coriandrum sativum.** Water medium; dislikes constant moisture; bolts if too dry at the seedling stage (P). Sun, part shade for leaf. H5 (R: /4371); autumn sowings overwinter under a cloche (R). October to November: summer plants have bolted (feathery leaves, white umbels, seed) and die; autumn sowings are small rosettes. Problems: bolting, aphids, powdery mildew, slugs (R).

**Ferns.** Dryopteris filix-mas H7 deciduous; Polystichum setiferum H7 evergreen (R: /114462, /13490). Shade, moist but drained, crown protected from winter wet. October to November: Dryopteris fronds brown and collapse (normal); Polystichum stays green while old fronds wither but stay attached (W). Brown tips mean too dry; vine weevil, slugs (R). Cut old fronds before spring crosiers.

**Japanese maple, Acer palmatum.** H6 (R: /225); prone to die in drought and will not survive waterlogged soil; USDA 5 to 8 (W). October to November: yellow to orange to red then leaf drop, normal (R). Problems: leaf scorch (brown crisp margins) from wind, sun or dry compost; verticillium wilt; vine weevil; scale; phytophthora root rot (R). Protect pot roots from freezing.

## Gaps with no open-licence source

- RHS H ratings and the full scale exist only on rhs.org.uk. Store each rating as a bare fact with the URL; describe the scale in our own words.
- Several problem lists and symptom one-liners (capsid, leafhopper, celery leaf miner, blue mint beetle, maple scorch) are RHS-only. Paraphrase with "source: RHS"; never copy sentences.
- Pot-specific winter advice (rain shadow, wrapping pots, the −8 °C rosemary figure) is RHS-only; PFAF gives embeddable equivalents, prefer those numbers.
- Crop coefficients: only mint has an open-standard figure. Basil and rosemary come from journal papers (cite the number with the URL). None found for sage, thyme, oregano, parsley, coriander, lemon balm, tarragon or chives; Pereira et al. 2024, Irrigation Science, doi:10.1007/s00271-024-00960-4 tabulates herbs but is paywalled.
- US extension and public-domain pages (USDA, UC IPM, Penn State) could not be reached in this sweep and remain unchecked.
