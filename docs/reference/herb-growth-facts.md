# Herb growth facts from published experiments

Quantitative numbers for the twin: cardinal temperatures, light, growth rate, water use and
deficit response. Swept 3 Oct 2026 through Europe PMC and OpenAlex. Most figures come from
abstracts; three papers were read in full (PMC10688745, PMC7993832, PMC10514919). Only
basil, parsley, sage and spearmint have usable growth-model numbers. Almost nothing exists
on frost tolerance, per-plant ET or full BBCH scales for any herb. "Derived" marks
arithmetic on published values.

## Basil (best covered)

- **Temperature.** Base 10.9 to 12.1 °C across four basil types (Walters and Currey 2019, HortScience 54:1915, doi:10.21273/hortsci12976-18). Fresh weight rises to 29 °C and falls at 35 °C. Optimum 25 to 30 °C (Chang et al. 2005) or 29 to 35 °C (Walters and Currey). Modelled optimum mean daily temperature 32.6 to 35.5 °C as DLI rises from 6 to 11 (Walters and Lopez 2023, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0294905).
- **Chilling.** Storage below 12 °C injures leaves; below 10 °C severe (review, doi:10.3389/fpls.2023.1237577). Postharvest data.
- **Light.** Optimum DLI 11.8 to 13.2 (Walters and Lopez 2023). Dou et al. 2018 (doi:10.21273/hortsci12785-17) recommend 12.9; shoot fresh weight 54% higher at 12.9 and 79% higher at 16.5 than at 9.3. Indoors, DLI 10 gave the most growth of 4 to 10 tested (doi:10.21273/horttech04442-19).
- **Flowering.** Day-neutral at DLI 7; facultative long-day at DLI 13 to 23; high DLI hastens flowering (doi:10.21273/hortsci18645-25).
- **Growth rate.** Node appearance 0.03 to 0.30 nodes a day between 11 and 29 °C (Walters and Currey). Marketable 3 weeks after transplant in hydroponics. Germination: 50% at 6.5 days, 90% at 11.7 days (doi:10.3390/agronomy12112694).
- **Water.** Lysimeter ETc 4.8 to 9.4 mm a day, 471 mm over 49 days, Kc 1.5 to 2.8 in Brazil (doi:10.1590/s0102-053620190402; that Kc looks inflated). Mashhad: 3.4 rising to 8.6 mm a day, Kc max 1.42 (doi:10.1556/446.13.2017.4). Colombia: peak 7.4 mm a day, Kc 0.75 / 0.98 / 0.76 (doi:10.3389/fsoil.2025.1621669).
- **Deficit.** Yield response factor Ky 0.70 to 0.76; 70% of field capacity gave no significant yield loss (doi:10.2166/ws.2021.134).
- **BBCH.** Only an adapted germination-stage scale.

## Mint

- **Spearmint.** Optimum mean daily temperature 30.4 falling to 27.8 °C as DLI rises from 6 to 18; DLI did not saturate up to 19 (Walters and Lopez 2023); a thesis gives 14.9. Marketable 3 weeks after transplanting rooted cuttings.
- **Peppermint water.** Kc 0.3 initial, 0.85 mid-season, 0.5 at harvest; optimum 324 to 487 mm a season; 89% of ET is transpiration (doi:10.2136/sssaj1998.03615995006200050037x). Sustained deficit caused stand loss (doi:10.13031/2013.42084).
- **Spearmint deficit.** 40% ETc all season: 47.2 vs 61.2 Mg per ha hay, a 23% loss (doi:10.1061/(asce)ir.1943-4774.0000719).
- **Shade.** Heavy shade did not reduce mint biomass, unlike the other herbs tested (doi:10.3390/plants14152428).
- **Photoperiod.** Long days enhance peppermint growth (doi:10.1104/pp.42.1.20).

## Parsley

- **Temperature.** Base 10.4 °C for fresh mass, 7.1 °C for leaf number. Fresh mass rose 13-fold from 10.0 to 22.4 °C, then fell 29% at 27.1 °C; fitted optimum 28 °C. Fv/Fm declines below 13.9 °C (Walters and Lopez 2021, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0248662).
- **Light.** No DLI effect between 6.2 and 16.9 in that study; +120% fresh mass at DLI 18 vs 7 in another (doi:10.3390/agronomy9070389).
- **Harvest.** 4 weeks after transplant, after 5 weeks of propagation.
- Water, frost, BBCH: not found. Petropoulos 2008 (doi:10.1016/j.scienta.2007.10.008) covers water deficit; abstract not retrievable.

## Coriander

- **Light.** +154% fresh mass at DLI 18 vs 7 (doi:10.3390/agronomy9070389).
- **Bolting.** In hot weather and long days; field harvest 7 to 11 weeks after seeding (doi:10.21273/hortsci14614-19). No numeric threshold.
- **Water.** Seasonal ETc 139.8 mm, mean Kc 0.87 (doi:10.1590/s0102-053620180404). Seed yield best at 0.8 ET (doi:10.56093/ijas.v89i7.91691).

## Sage

- **Temperature.** Optimum mean daily temperature 27.5 falling to 23.5 °C as DLI rises from 6 to 18. Fresh mass 37 to 72 g and height 9 to 18 cm between 18 and 27 °C (Walters and Lopez 2023).
- **Light.** Optimum DLI 17.9 falling to 14.4 as temperature rises from 24 to 32 °C. Heavy shade cut biomass 40 to 49% across sage, rosemary, thyme and lavender (doi:10.3390/plants14152428).
- **Harvest.** 5 weeks after transplant. Potted plants reached 51 cm and 51.5 g fresh herb under regular irrigation (doi:10.1016/j.sjbs.2021.08.034).
- **BBCH.** Germination stages only (doi:10.15835/nbha52213676).

## Rosemary, thyme, oregano

- **Rosemary.** "Minimal impact on biomass" under controlled drought (doi:10.1016/j.indcrop.2024.119315, title only). Best herbage at an irrigation to pan-evaporation ratio of 0.50 (doi:10.1080/14620316.2004.11511782).
- **Thyme.** Total irrigation use 5.85 L per plant per season at 20 kPa soil tension (doi:10.22004/ag.econ.258905).
- **Oregano** (Origanum onites, not O. vulgare). Ky 1.06 to 1.13, "slightly sensitive" (doi:10.1002/jsfa.10703).

## Lemon balm, tarragon, chives

- **Lemon balm.** 30% vs 70% soil water capacity reduced height, bush diameter and mass; no percentages in the abstract (doi:10.13080/z-a.2016.103.049).
- **Tarragon.** French tarragon lines differ in winter survival "at the limit of survival"; no numbers (doi:10.1080/14620316.2008.11512458).
- **Chives.** For the asatsuki variety, leaf growth is best at 17 to 25 °C and sprouting is inhibited at 25 °C or above; summer-dormant (doi:10.2503/jjshs.56.60).
- **Garlic chives.** Nothing quantitative found.

## What this means for the twin

- **Growth is temperature-limited in a London autumn.** Basil's base temperature is about 11 to 12 °C and parsley's about 7 to 10 °C; Heathrow's mean October minimum is 8.8 °C. Expect near-zero basil growth from October, which matches the care pack ("basil is finishing").
- **Light is the second limit.** These herbs want a DLI of 12 to 18; a London October day delivers far less, and a shaded patio less again. Mint is the exception: heavy shade did not reduce it.
- **Drought sensitivity ranks the herbs.** Rosemary barely responds, basil loses about a third of yield at half water, oregano about half, and mint suffers stand loss under sustained deficit. That ranking should set per-species "water now" thresholds.
- **Well studied:** basil; spearmint, sage and parsley for temperature and light; peppermint for field water use. **Poorly studied:** rosemary, thyme, oregano, lemon balm, tarragon, chives, garlic chives. For those, the twin uses ECOCROP ranges and the care packs, tier C.
- **Best sources:** Walters and Currey 2019 for basil temperature; Walters and Lopez 2023 for spearmint and sage; Walters and Lopez 2021 for parsley; a 2026 systematic review of 60 basil lighting studies exists as a preprint (doi:10.20944/preprints202609.2555.v1).
